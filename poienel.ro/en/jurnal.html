Marking out the ground for the November planting, and a climb up the hill at Crucea

17 October 2026 Landscaping Leisure

We carried on marking out the ground for the planting in November. With the tape stretched along the line and lath stakes driven in at the spacings from the plan, we marked the rows and positions on the cleared plot, along the hedge at the edge; the day was overcast and cold, just right for work.

In the afternoon we drove up the hill at Crucea, Allah Bair Hill, on the dirt track through the black pine plantation. On top, dry steppe grass, hawthorns and dog roses heavy with red fruit, rusty smoke-tree bushes and the view across the valley to the ridges around. It is the place where you best see what the forest-steppe we are imitating in the Miyawaki forest means.

Well-rotted farmyard manure on the Miyawaki forest plot

8 October 2026 Landscaping

We brought well-rotted farmyard manure and tipped it in heaps, from the pickup, on the plot in the north-east corner where the Miyawaki forest is to be planted. The manure is well fermented, dark, with no strong smell: exactly what the method's layer of organic matter calls for.

Then we spread it with the tractor over the whole area to be planted, between the stakes marking the outline, so it is worked into the soil at the loosening before planting. On our humus-poor steppe, this layer is the investment that decides how fast the saplings get going in the first two years.

General clearing: with the backhoe and by hand, with local help

2 October 2026 Landscaping

We did the general clearing of the ground, the preparation before marking out and the November planting. The backhoe pulled out the old bushes, the roots and the tall weeds and levelled the areas to be planted, and where it could not get in, among the trees we are keeping and along the fence, we cleared by hand, with a helper from the village, with the wheelbarrow and hand tools.

By the end of the day the ground was clean soil, with the tracks of the machine still fresh, from the house to the bottom of the garden. Next steps: the farmyard manure on the Miyawaki forest plot, then marking out the rows with the tape and stakes.

The first snowfall caught at the new project

19 February 2026 Leisure

The first snowfall we caught at Crucea: the yard white, the sky clear and cold, the house under snow, and the young spruces in front of it the only green spots. On the Dobrogea steppe snow does not last long, so we came to see it.

A day for standing and looking, not for work: the ground is frozen and last year's vegetable garden sleeps under the cover. Slowly melting snow is the best water for the trees we plant in autumn.

The grandparents try a vegetable garden: a big, ambitious project

18 May 2025 Landscaping

The grandparents have set up a vegetable garden on the back plot: long rows laid out with string, of onions, lettuce, radishes and greens, with the watering hose run alongside them. It is a big, ambitious project for the first summer at Crucea, on ground that had not been worked for years.

The vegetable garden is also the first test of the soil and water here: how well it holds moisture, how fast it dries out in summer, what grows well on our steppe. What we learn from these rows goes into the plan for the fruit trees, the shrubs and the Miyawaki forest.

The cherry plum is in bloom

16 March 2025 Leisure

The old cherry plum by the gate has flowered, all white, over the stone wall and above the roof: the first tree in the yard to tell us spring has come to Crucea. On the steppe it opens the season, two or three weeks ahead of the other fruit trees.

The cherry plum (Prunus cerasifera) stays in the garden plan, as a fruit tree and as a marker in the phenology calendar: when it flowers, we start the spring works.

Friends visiting, and the walk to the spring on the hill for water

2 February 2025 Leisure

We had friends visiting at Crucea, children included. We spent time in the yard, with a small fire by the shed, and in the house, where the little ones took over the bed in the green-walled room.

In the afternoon we all walked up the hill to the stone-built spring below the little wood, to fill bottles with water. From the top you see the whole valley, with the fields and the hills around; the trees were still bare, but the first tiny flowers were showing beneath them. The group's first outing on our hills.

The day we got the keys to the house at Crucea

20 January 2025 Milestones

We completed the purchase and received the keys to the little house at Crucea, the one that would become the Poienel garden. A stone house with green shutters, a stone wall towards the lane and a steppe yard, the ground still asleep under winter; cold January sun.

The photo was taken by the lady who sold us the house, on that very day, with the two of us, George and Ana, the new owners, in front of the gate. Everything starts here: the plans, the species, the Miyawaki forest and this journal.
