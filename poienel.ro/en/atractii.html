Sights around Poienel

What to see within an hour of Crucea, Constanța: monasteries, Danube fortresses, fossil hills and limestone gorges

About the area

Poienel is in Crucea, Constanța County, in northern Dobrogea, Romania, between the Danube and the Casimcea valley, on the DN2A Constanța–Hârșova road. The area is steppe on Jurassic limestone, with low hills full of fossils, Roman fortresses on the bank of the Danube and monasteries built beside springs and caves. The list below starts from the nearest sights, in Crucea commune, and reaches the Dobrogea Gorges; distances are by road, approximate, from Poienel.

11 sights: 3 nature, 3 religious, 3 history, 1 culture, 1 scenery.

Crucea Monastery

ReligiousBăltăgești, Crucea commune, Constanța County · about 4 km from Poienel

A convent of the Archdiocese of Tomis, built from 2005 next to the old pilgrimage spring at Crucea, with a wooden church in Maramureș style.

The monastery was founded on 1 July 2005 by the Archdiocese of Tomis, on the spot where people from the area had come to the spring for generations. Its organisation and administration were entrusted to Mother Teodora Tăbăranu, the abbess; the community numbers a few nuns.

The wooden church, in Maramureș style, was begun in August 2005, finished in 2007 and consecrated in 2009 by Archbishop Teodosie of Tomis. Next to it stands a wooden chapel, and a new masonry church is being built inside the precinct.

The monastery's feast days are the Life-giving Spring (the Friday of Bright Week, after Easter), Saint Theodora of Sihla (7 August) and the Exaltation of the Holy Cross (14 September). The feast of the Life-giving Spring draws the most pilgrims, continuing the pre-war custom.

Practical

  • About 4 km from the village of Crucea, on the left side of the DN2A Constanța–Hârșova road, between Crucea and Băltăgești; it is signposted from the road and reached by a short track.
  • Visiting is free, in daylight hours; modest dress is expected. Services follow the usual monastic schedule, with the Divine Liturgy on Sundays and feast days.

Sources: Arhiepiscopia Tomisului — Mănăstirea Crucea · Ziarul Lumina — Mănăstirea „Sfânta Cruce”, izvor de apă vindecătoare · CreștinOrtodox.ro — Mănăstirea Crucea

The Healing Spring at Crucea

ReligiousBăltăgești, Crucea commune, Constanța County · about 4 km from Poienel

The spring, laid out in the 1930s and held to be healing, around which first the pilgrimage and then the monastery grew.

The spring lies inside the precinct of Crucea Monastery and is older than it. Between the wars it was laid out by a priest from Băltăgești together with the villagers, and every year, on the Friday of Bright Week, the faithful from the surrounding villages gathered beside it for a service; the custom also gave the monastery its main feast.

Locals consider the water clean and good to drink, and tradition credits it with healing powers. The spring is tapped and has a small fountain where pilgrims fill bottles to take home.

Practical

  • Access through the monastery courtyard, any day; bring containers if you want to take water.
  • In a steppe region poor in water, the spring was for centuries a landmark for the villages and for the flocks of the area.

Sources: Ziarul Lumina — Mănăstirea „Sfânta Cruce”, izvor de apă vindecătoare · Vatra — Mănăstirea Crucea, vechi loc de pelerinaj

Allah Bair Hill Nature Reserve

NatureBăltăgești, Crucea commune, Constanța County · about 6 km from Poienel

A 205 m hill of Jurassic limestone, a 10 ha geological, palaeontological and botanical reserve, with reef fossils and some 30 rare plant species.

Allah Bair Hill (205 m) is part of the Băltăgești ridge and the highest point around. It was declared a nature reserve of national interest by Law no. 5/2000 (code 2.367), on 10 ha; around it stretch the Natura 2000 sites ROSCI0053 Dealul Allah Bair (194 ha) and ROSPA0002 Allah Bair–Capidava, a special protection area for birds.

The hill's limestone formed on the floor of a Jurassic sea, from coral reefs, and is full of fossils: from protozoa to echinoderms and vertebrates. That is why the reserve is first of all geological and palaeontological.

About 30 rare plant species grow on the limestone outcrops, among them the endemic Dobrogean bellflower (Campanula romanica). The name comes from Turkish (Allah Bayır), translated locally either as "God's Hill" or "Priest's Hill".

Practical

  • The hill is visible from Băltăgești; you walk up on pasture tracks, there is no marked trail and no facilities. Good shoes, water and a hat: there is no shade in summer.
  • It is a protected area: no picking plants, no breaking rocks for fossils, no fires. Spring (April–May) is the finest season, when the rock plants flower.

Sources: Lista rezervațiilor naturale din județul Constanța (Wikipedia) · Săptămâna Verde — Dealul Allah Bair · Descoperă Dobrogea — Rezervația Allah Bair · Ordinul MMAP nr. 1252/2016 — planul de management

Carsium Fortress and the Carsium Museum, Hârșova

HistoryHârșova, the town of Hârșova, Constanța County · about 18 km from Poienel

A Roman fortress raised on the high bank of the Danube over a Getic settlement, in use until Ottoman times; a museum founded in 1904 by King Carol I.

Carsium was built by the Romans in stone and brick on the site of a Getic settlement, on the high bank of the Danube, as part of the Danube limes. It was rebuilt by the Byzantines and, much later, turned into an Ottoman fortress, hence the walls still visible today on the cliff.

The Carsium Museum was inaugurated in 1904 by King Carol I, reopened in 1926 by King Ferdinand and revived in 2006; today it is a branch of the National Museum of History and Archaeology in Constanța and is open at weekends too.

The fortress was restored with European funds and opened to visitors in 2025, with a museum trail and a Roman building with a mosaic. In summer the museum runs an archaeology summer school at the fortress.

Practical

  • Hârșova is about 18 km from Crucea on the DN2A. The fortress stands on the Danube cliff, near the port; the museum is in town, in a house with a veranda.
  • Also here: the view over the Danube and the wetlands on the far bank, and the canarale, the rocky limestone banks that give their name to the Natura 2000 site "Canaralele Dunării".

Sources: Adevărul — Hârșova, cetate otomană · Spotmedia — Cetatea Carsium își deschide porțile · Cercetări arheologice — The Roman frontier in Dobrudja: Hârșova (Carsium)

Ghindărești, the Lipovan village on the Danube

SceneryGhindărești, Ghindărești commune, Constanța County · about 22 km from Poienel

A village of Russian Lipovans, Danube fishermen, with an Old Rite church, boats on the shore and the high bank of the river.

Ghindărești is a village of Russian Lipovans (Old Believers) on the right bank of the Danube, south of Hârșova. The community has kept its language, its festive dress, its Old Rite church and its trade as fishermen; the village is known for the boats on the shore and for the fish from the Danube wetlands.

From the high bank you see the Danube with its islands, and at sunset the light on the water is the reason many people stop here on the way to Capidava.

Practical

  • On the DJ223, between Hârșova and Topalu; about 22 km from Crucea. In the village, respect the rules of the Old Rite church (do not enter during services without the locals' agreement, take photographs discreetly).
  • If you want fresh fish, ask the fishermen in the morning.

Sources: Wikipedia — Ghindărești

The "Dinu and Sevasta Vintilă" Art Museum, Topalu

CultureTopalu, Topalu commune, Constanța County · about 25 km from Poienel

A museum of Romanian art in a village on the Danube, born from the collection donated by Dr Gheorghe Vintilă in memory of his parents.

The museum opened in 1961 in the native village of Dr Gheorghe Vintilă, who donated his art collection in memory of his parents, Dinu and Sevasta. The collection comprises Romanian painting, graphic art and sculpture from the 19th and 20th centuries, with works by important artists of the period.

It is one of the few art museums in rural Romania and belongs to the network of the National Museum of History and Archaeology in Constanța, which opens it free of charge on "Museum Night in the Villages".

Practical

  • Topalu is on the DJ223, on the bank of the Danube, about 25 km from Crucea; the museum is in the centre of the village. Check the opening hours beforehand: it runs with limited staff.
  • It combines naturally with Capidava Fortress, a few km further south.

Sources: G4Media — Noaptea muzeelor la sate: Capidava, Adamclisi, Histria și Topalu

Capidava Fortress

HistoryCapidava, Topalu commune, Constanța County · about 30 km from Poienel

A Roman fort on the right bank of the Danube, inhabited for over a thousand years, from Trajan to the Middle Ages; walls, bastions and buildings on view, with historical re-enactments in summer.

Capidava was raised by the Romans on the right bank of the Danube, between Cernavodă and Hârșova, as part of the limes. The site has layers from prehistory, the early and late Roman periods, the Byzantine period and the Middle Ages: it was in use for over a millennium.

You can see the curtain walls, the bastions, the gate and military and civil buildings, among them the late Roman principia (headquarters), excavated since 2013. The fortress was restored with European funds, with works completed in 2015, and is run by the National Museum of History and Archaeology in Constanța.

In summer it hosts the history students' field camp and, from time to time, the historical re-enactment show "At the Edge of the Empire".

Practical

  • From Crucea take the DN2A towards Hârșova, then the DJ223 along the Danube through Ghindărești and Topalu; about 30 km. Opening hours and tickets are set by the museum in Constanța.
  • On the way, at Topalu, the art museum is worth a stop (see below).

Sources: G4Media — Turul cetăților: Capidava, Adamclisi, Histria, Topalu · Radio Constanța — „La Marginea Imperiului” la Cetatea Capidava · Cronica cercetărilor arheologice — Capidava

Ulmetum Fortress, Pantelimon

HistoryPantelimon de Sus, Pantelimon commune, Constanța County · about 35 km from Poienel

A late Roman fortress from the end of the 4th century, rebuilt by Justinian in the 6th century, in the Casimcea valley; ruins excavated by Vasile Pârvan.

Ulmetum was built at the end of the 4th century as a Roman fortification in the province of Scythia Minor, on an inland road linking the Danube to the coast. Abandoned after the Hun attacks, it was partly rebuilt in the 6th century, in Justinian's time, and finally deserted at the start of the 7th century.

The fortress was excavated by Vasile Pârvan between 1911 and 1914, one of the first major archaeological digs in Dobrogea; research was resumed by the National Museum of History and Archaeology in Constanța. The curtain walls with towers and the traces of the buildings are visible.

Practical

  • Pantelimon de Sus is about 35 km from Crucea, on the road to Târgușor; the fortress is at the edge of the village, on a plateau above the valley. Visiting is free, with no facilities.
  • It combines with Saint John Cassian Monastery and the Dobrogea Gorges, in the same direction.

Sources: Wikipedia — Ulmetum (castra) · Cronica cercetărilor arheologice — Ulmetum

Saint John Cassian Monastery and Cave

ReligiousCasian, Târgușor commune, Constanța County · about 35 km from Poienel

A monastery of white Dobrogea stone, built from 2001 on Casian Hill, and the cave in the limestone cliff where tradition says Saint John Cassian, born here around 360, lived as a hermit.

Saint John Cassian was born around 360 in Scythia Minor, lived in Egypt, Constantinople and Rome, founded two monasteries in Marseille and died there in 435. Tradition links his birthplace to Casian Hill, above the Casimcea valley.

The monastery was built from 2001, under Abbot Iustin Petre, and the old monastic settlement was officially revived on 28 February 2003, with the blessing of Archbishop Teodosie. The church is of white limestone, the local material, and the precinct has guest houses and a refectory.

The cave lies on the north slope of Casian Hill, in a limestone wall; the entrance is a few metres above the ground and is reached by a metal stair. Inside, candle niches and old inscriptions survive.

Practical

  • From Crucea, via Pantelimon towards Târgușor, about 35 km; from the monastery, the path to the cave starts behind the precinct, crosses an orchard and drops into a fairly steep valley, among rocks and scrub: it needs good shoes and a little fitness.
  • The feast of Saint John Cassian is on 29 February (28 February in non-leap years).

Sources: Financiarul — Mănăstirea și Peștera Sfântului Ioan Casian · Agerpres — Sărbători religioase, 28 februarie

The Dobrogea Gorges and the Jurassic Reefs of Cheia

NatureCheia, Grădina commune, Constanța County · about 40 km from Poienel

Gorges cut through Jurassic coral reefs more than 150 million years old, a nature reserve without facilities, a place for hiking, climbing and birdwatching.

The rocks at Cheia are former coral reefs of the Tethys Sea, from the Jurassic, later uplifted and cut by the Casimcea river. The Jurassic Reefs of Cheia nature reserve (code 2.362) lies within the Natura 2000 site ROSCI0215 and the ROSPA0019 Cheile Dobrogei special protection area for birds; the reserve covers almost 300 ha.

Steppe and rock plants grow on the limestone, and birds of prey nest in the walls; in spring the gorges are full of steppe peonies and dwarf irises. There is no marked trail and no infrastructure: you park by the roadside and walk freely.

Practical

  • From Crucea, via Pantelimon and Târgușor, then towards Cheia; about 40 km. The same road leads to Gura Dobrogei.
  • Protected area: no fires, no picking plants, no vehicles off the road. Finest in April–May and in September.

Sources: Ministerul Mediului — Regulamentul ariei protejate Cheile Dobrogei (PDF) · Dobrogea Explore — Ce să faci în Cheile Dobrogei · Economedia — Recifurile transformate în Cheile Dobrogei

The Bat Cave at Gura Dobrogei

NatureGura Dobrogei, Cogealac commune, Constanța County · about 45 km from Poienel

A cave in Jurassic limestone, a nature reserve, with large bat colonies and traces of habitation from the Neolithic to Roman times; reported by Vasile Pârvan in 1926.

The cave opens in the Jurassic limestone north-east of the village of Gura Dobrogei, in the Casimcea valley. It was first reported by Vasile Pârvan in 1926, and excavations have revealed Neolithic, Greek and Roman habitation. It is a nature reserve (Law 5/2000), together with the nearby La Adam Cave.

The name comes from the bat colonies, among the largest in Dobrogea, with protected species; visit with care, without noise and without shining bright lights at the ceiling.

Practical

  • From the car park it is a 10–15 minute climb. One of the entrances is a pothole, not recommended; the second entrance is the one used, but it needs a head torch and shoes with grip. Do not enter during the bats' hibernation (November–March).
  • About 45 km from Crucea, in the same direction as the Dobrogea Gorges.

Sources: Financiarul — Peștera Liliecilor, monument natural în Dobrogea · Adevărul — Batman în Dobrogea
